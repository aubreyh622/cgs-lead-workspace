## Post-merger integration planning

We help mid-size manufacturers plan the first year after an acquisition or merger closes. The work decides which processes and systems to combine first, which to leave alone for now, and how to keep plants shipping while the change happens.

Clients usually call us when two businesses that now share one owner still run separate ERP systems, separate engineering processes and separate quality standards, and leadership has promised the board savings that depend on bringing them together.

The integration is often led by a newly appointed integration lead or Head of Transformation, working with the COO and the CFO. The first months after closing are when they decide whether to build the plan themselves or bring in help.

In a recent engagement, a motion-control manufacturer that had merged with a competitor used our plan to move nine plants onto one order-to-cash process in eleven months, without a shipping delay.

## Scaling AI pilots into production

Many manufacturers have run AI pilots for predictive maintenance, visual inspection or demand forecasting at one plant for a year or more, and the pilots work, but nobody owns the plan to roll them out to the other sites.

We help leadership choose which pilots to scale first, size the value of each, and set up the operating model behind them: who owns each use case, how a pilot becomes a standard, and which data and skills each plant needs before it can take one on.

This need usually becomes visible when a company appoints its first Chief Digital Officer or Chief Data Officer, or when an executive says in public that the pilots work but the company lacks a plan to scale them.

## Operating model design for multi-site operations

When several plants or regions do the same work in different ways, costs rise and decisions slow down. We map how work flows today, agree a target operating model with leadership, and plan the transition: structure, decision rights, governance and shared services.

Typical triggers are a regional consolidation, a new shared-services center, a reorganization announced by a new COO, or a growth phase in which the company has added plants faster than it has standardized how they run.

## Finance transformation

Finance teams at growing manufacturers often close the books slowly because each business unit reconciles by hand and reports in its own format. After an acquisition, the problem doubles.

We redesign the close, reporting and planning processes and the finance operating model, and plan the move alongside any system change. The sponsor is usually the CFO or the corporate controller, sometimes newly hired to professionalize finance before a sale or an IPO.

Public signs include a newly appointed CFO with a transformation mandate, a delayed or restated earnings report, a planned listing, or a private-equity owner preparing the company for sale.

## Sales and operations planning

Manufacturers facing volatile demand, tariff changes or supply disruptions often find that sales, operations and finance each plan with different numbers. Inventory grows in some places while customers wait in others.

We set up a monthly sales and operations planning process with one agreed demand and supply plan, clear decision rights and the reports leadership needs to run it. The work is usually sponsored by the COO or a VP of Supply Chain.

Companies tend to look for help after a profit warning that blames inventory or supply chain costs, after a large customer or product line is added, or when they open a new distribution center or plant.

## New plant and capacity expansion readiness

Opening a new plant or a major expansion is a chance to set up processes, systems and roles the right way from the start, and a risk if the new site simply copies the habits of the old ones.

We help operations leaders plan the start-up: the operating model of the new site, the systems it runs on, the staffing plan and the ramp-up milestones. Engagements usually begin between the announcement of a new plant and the start of construction.
